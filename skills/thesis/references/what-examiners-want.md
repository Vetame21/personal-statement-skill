# What Examiners Want

There is no single national or international rubric: every program sets its own weights. Across published rubrics (Indonesian informatics defense rubrics, and computer science project descriptors from universities such as UNSW, Liverpool and Sussex), the same criteria keep appearing. Always ask the student for their program's rubric and score against that first.

---

## 1. Criteria that appear everywhere

| Criterion | Average thesis | Excellent thesis |
|---|---|---|
| **Problem** | Broad, vague, "important" because the student says so | Narrow, real, evidenced; the reader understands why it matters in two sentences |
| **Literature / theory** | Summary of sources, one after another | Critical and organised by theme; shows what is known, what is not, and where this thesis fits |
| **Method** | Chosen because it is common; steps listed | Justified against alternatives; repeatable; assumptions checked |
| **Data / implementation quality** | Small or convenience data, no checks; system works on the happy path | Clean, documented data; tested system; evaluation against a baseline |
| **Results** | Tables without explanation | Clear figures and tables, each one read for the reader |
| **Discussion** | Restates results | Explains why, compares with prior studies, admits limitations and threats to validity |
| **Contribution** | Unclear | Stated explicitly; modest is fine if it is honest and real |
| **Writing & structure** | Repetitive, inconsistent terms, spelling errors | Logical flow, consistent terms, clean language |
| **Referencing** | Missing or mismatched citations, mixed styles | Every source cited and listed correctly in one style |
| **Defense** | Reads slides, cannot explain choices | Explains every choice and number, handles critical questions calmly |

Example weights, as summarised from one Indonesian informatics defense rubric ([Universitas Mataram, Informatika](https://if.unram.ac.id/wp-content/uploads/2025/12/Rubrik-Penilaian-Kedalaman-Sidang-Tugas-Akhir.pdf); the document itself could not be opened when this was written): problem complexity, depth of theory, methodological fit and data quality at about 15% each, with the rest on presentation and defense. Treat this as an example only.

What top bands add (from international descriptors): **originality** (going beyond what was taught), **critical engagement** with current research, **realistic appraisal of achievements and limitations**, and work close to publishable or professional quality.

## 3. Most common mistakes

**Bab I / Introduction**
- Background full of theory and definitions (move to Bab II).
- Background that never states the gap.
- Research questions that cannot be answered with the planned data, or are yes/no questions with no analysis behind them.
- Objectives that do not match the research questions one to one.
- "Manfaat" paragraphs longer than the problem statement.

**Bab II / Literature**
- A list of summaries ("Menurut A... Menurut B...") with no comparison or synthesis.
- Old or irrelevant sources while recent key studies are missing.
- Theories introduced and never used again.
- Citing Wikipedia, blogs, or AI output as academic sources.

**Bab III / Method**
- Method chosen without justification.
- Sample size or sampling method not explained.
- Statistical tests run without checking their assumptions.
- No evaluation plan for a system or model (how will success be measured?).

**Bab IV / Results**
- Results and interpretation mixed so the reader cannot tell what was found.
- Claiming "signifikan" without a significance test.
- Reporting only good results; ignoring failures or odd cases.
- Screenshots of the whole system instead of evidence that it meets the requirements.
- No comparison with prior studies.

**Bab V / Conclusion**
- New results appearing for the first time.
- Conclusions that do not answer the research questions.
- Generic suggestions.

**Throughout**
- Inconsistent terms (switching between "model", "sistem", "aplikasi" for the same thing).
- Spelling and punctuation errors (the most frequent language error in studies of Indonesian theses).
- Figures and tables not referenced in the text.
- Citation and reference list mismatches.
- Text that reads as machine-written (see the tone rules in SKILL.md).
