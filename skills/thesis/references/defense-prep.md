# Defense Preparation (Seminar Proposal, Seminar Hasil, Sidang, 答辩)

## 1. What the defense tests

Whether the student **understands and owns** the work: why the problem, why this method, what the numbers mean, what the limits are. Examiners often probe the weakest chapter, and anything that looks copied or AI-generated.

## 2. Slides (12-18 for a 15-20 minute talk; check the time limit)

1. Title, name, supervisor(s)
2. Problem and why it matters (one number or case)
3. Research questions / objectives
4. Key prior work and the gap (one comparison table)
5. Method (one diagram)
6. Data or system overview
7-10. Results (one main message per slide, the number in the slide title)
11. Discussion: comparison with prior work
12. Limitations
13. Conclusions (one per research question)
14. Future work / suggestions
15. Thank you + backup slides (extra results, detailed tables, code architecture)

Rules: few words per slide, readable charts, every number on a slide is one the student can explain.

## 3. Question bank

Prepare a 3-4 sentence answer for each, grounded in the thesis.

**Problem and contribution**
- Why this topic? Why now? Who benefits?
- What exactly is new in your work?
- If you had to summarise your contribution in one sentence?

**Literature**
- Which study is closest to yours, and how is yours different?
- Why didn't you use method X that study Y used?

**Method**
- Why this method and not an alternative?
- How did you choose the sample / data / parameters?
- How do you know your instrument or measurement is valid and reliable?
- Did you check the assumptions of the statistical test?

**Results**
- Explain this table / figure. What does this number mean?
- Is the difference significant? How do you know?
- Why did result Z happen? What surprised you?

**Limitations and validity**
- What are the weaknesses of your study?
- What would change your conclusion?
- How would you do it differently with more time?

**Implementation (system or model projects)**
- Walk through this part of the code / architecture.
- How did you test it? What happens with bad input?
- Which parts did you write yourself, and which used libraries or AI assistance?

**Integrity**
- How did you use AI tools, and how did you check their output?

## 4. Mock defense

Run a mock defense as the panel from `debate-panel.md`: the Examiner asks 8-10 questions, mixing friendly and hard ones; after each answer give one line of feedback (clear / unclear / incorrect + a better answer outline). End with the three answers that most need practice.

## 5. On the day

- Answer the question asked; if unsure, say what you know and what you would check.
- It is fine to say "that is a limitation of this study", followed by how it could be addressed.
- Note every revision the examiners request, and confirm them at the end.
