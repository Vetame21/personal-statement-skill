# Openings and Closings

The first two sentences decide how carefully the rest is read; the last two decide what the reader remembers. All examples below are invented to show the technique. Use the applicant's real material.

## Openings

| Technique | How it works | Example |
|---|---|---|
| **The small scene** | Drop the reader into one concrete moment | "My cousin coded on the monitor next to my desk because he had no laptop. I watched." |
| **The object** | One physical thing that carries the story | "The first thing I ever built was a website about a TV show, and it had a broken menu for two weeks." |
| **The question you actually had** | A real question the applicant asked, not a rhetorical one | "When my aunt's shop switched to QR payments, I wanted to know who decided a payment was safe." |
| **The contrast** | Expectation vs reality | "Our school taught programming in one editor and one textbook; I learned the rest from tutorials at night." |
| **The plain statement** | A clear, confident sentence with a fact | "I have spent two years building small Python programs that mostly broke." |

Avoid: dictionary definitions, famous quotes, "Ever since I was young...", rhetorical questions to the reader ("Have you ever wondered...?"), and broad claims about the world ("In today's digital age...").

## Closings

| Technique | How it works | Example |
|---|---|---|
| **The echo** | Return to the opening image, changed | "I used to watch someone else at the keyboard. Now I want to be the one typing." |
| **The next step** | End on the concrete first thing the applicant will do | "In my first year, I want to take statistics and machine learning together and build one fraud-detection project before my first internship." |
| **The contribution** | What the applicant will add to the program | "I will bring the habit I learned from broken code: test one step at a time, and ask for help early." |

Avoid: summaries ("In conclusion..."), pleading ("I really hope..."), and promises the applicant cannot keep ("I will change the world").

## Transitions between paragraphs

Each paragraph's first sentence should follow from the last one's end. Useful moves:

- **Cause → effect**: "That bug is why I started reading about how programs run."
- **Question → search**: "Looking for a degree that could take me further, I..."
- **Small → large**: "If a simple QR payment needs data science, so does the rest of banking."

Avoid starting several paragraphs with the same connector ("Furthermore", "In addition", "Selain itu").
