# Integrity, AI Use and Similarity

## 1. Generative AI rules

- **Indonesia:** In October 2024 the Ministry (then Kemendikbudristek) released the *Panduan Penggunaan Generative AI pada Pembelajaran di Perguruan Tinggi* (six chapters; ethics covering academic integrity, safety, fairness and transparency, and environmental impact). As reported in the media, it says GenAI should assist rather than write a thesis; lecturers should say when GenAI is allowed or not; and where allowed, students must document their use and give attribution. Read the official text (linked from the ministry announcement, s.id/PanduanGenAI) and the campus's own rules, which may be stricter.
- **Other countries / universities:** Rules vary from "not allowed" to "allowed with disclosure". Always ask, or check the program handbook.

### Usually acceptable (when the campus allows AI)
- Brainstorming topics and research questions, then checking them with the supervisor.
- Explaining concepts, methods or error messages.
- Feedback on structure and clarity of the student's own draft.
- Grammar and spelling checks.
- Help debugging code the student wrote and understands.

### Usually not acceptable
- Generating chapters or sections submitted as the student's own writing.
- Generating or "improving" data, results, interview quotes, or survey responses.
- Generating references.
- Paraphrasing other people's text to hide similarity.

### Disclosure template (adapt to the campus format)

> I used [tool, version] for [purpose: e.g. checking grammar in Chapters 2-4, explaining the assumptions of the Mann-Whitney test, debugging the data-cleaning script]. I reviewed and edited all output, and I take full responsibility for the content of this thesis.

Keep a short log (date, tool, what for, what was kept) in case the supervisor asks.

## 2. Similarity checks (Turnitin and others)

- Limits are set by each campus, sometimes each faculty. Published examples: maximum 25% for skripsi at one faculty of Universitas Airlangga (from 2025/2026), and a 20% maximum at UGM's Faculty of Cultural Sciences, with the supervisor making the final decision. Do not rely on numbers from blogs.
- Ask whether the bibliography, quotes and small matches are excluded from the count.
- A high score is fixed by proper quoting and citing, and by writing ideas in the student's own words. **Do not help with "spinning" text to beat the checker**; that is still plagiarism, and many checkers now also flag AI-paraphrased text.

## 3. Data and research ethics

- Get permission for data collection (institution permits, informed consent for surveys and interviews), and anonymise personal data.
- Keep raw data and analysis files; examiners may ask for them.
- For code: credit libraries, tutorials and any AI-generated code in the thesis or repository.
