# Interview Prep from a Personal Statement

First find out the format. If the user does not know, help them check the program's official admissions page, and do not guess.

| Format | What it means for prep |
|---|---|
| **Live interview** (in person or video call) | The interviewer usually has the statement and asks follow-ups on it. Prepare for depth on every claim. |
| **Recorded video, same questions for everyone** | Questions are generic and not based on the statement. Prepare a reusable story bank. |
| **Group interview / discussion** | Prepare a short self-introduction and 2-3 informed opinions on the field. |
| **No interview** | The statement carries more weight; skip this stage. |

When interviews are "on a selective basis", the written application (including the statement) usually decides who is invited. Say this to the user: it makes the statement more important, not less.

---

## 1. Live interview: claim-by-claim prep

For each concrete claim in the statement:

1. Write the likely follow-up question ("Tell me about the quiz game. What was hardest?").
2. Draft 3-4 bullet answer notes, not a script.
3. Flag any claim the applicant cannot answer comfortably. Fix the statement or prepare an honest answer.

Add the standard questions: why this course, why this university, why this city/country, strengths and weaknesses, a challenge, future plans, questions for the panel.

## 2. Recorded video: the story bank

Build 3-4 stories from the statement. Each one should:

- Be true and already in the statement (consistency matters if reviewers see both).
- Fit several common prompts.
- Be sayable in 60-90 seconds (about 130-200 spoken words).
- Follow a simple spine: **moment → what I did → what changed → link to the course**.

Typical generic prompts to map against (these are common examples, not a confirmed list for any university):

| Prompt type | Which story to use |
|---|---|
| Introduce yourself | Origin story |
| Why this program / field | Turning point + course fit |
| Why this university / city | Place story (with 1 verified fact) |
| A challenge you overcame | Obstacle + self-study story |
| Teamwork or leadership | Any story with other people in it |
| Future plans | Plan paragraph, in 3 steps |
| Something you are proud of | The thing they built or achieved |

Output a table mapping prompts to stories, then each story written as natural spoken English (short sentences, contractions, no lists), with a word count.

## 3. Delivery tips for recorded video

- Practise each story out loud; record once on a phone and watch it back.
- Do not read from a script on screen. Keep 3-4 keywords per story.
- Look at the camera lens, not the screen.
- Pause before answering; recorded systems usually give thinking time. Check the instructions.
- Quiet room, light in front of the face, plain background, stable device.
- If the system allows retakes, know how many before starting.

## 4. Consistency check

Before finishing, compare the stories against the statement. Same facts, same names, same reasons. Different wording is fine; different content is not.
