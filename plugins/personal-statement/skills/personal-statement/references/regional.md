# Regional Conventions

Rules change every admissions cycle. Treat everything below as a starting point and **always tell the user to confirm limits and format on the official portal** for their year of entry. If a search tool is available, check the current rules before giving numbers.

---

## Hong Kong (undergraduate)

- Two main routes: **JUPAS** (local secondary students) and **direct / non-JUPAS** applications (international and other applicants), each run through different portals.
- Whether a personal statement is required, and its length, varies by university and route. Third-party guides often quote roughly 300-500 words for JUPAS-style statements; verify for the specific program.
- Some programs use interviews **on a selective basis**, and some use **pre-recorded video interviews** with the same questions for every applicant. Check the university's interview arrangements page for the route.
- Readers value clear English, specific reasons for the program, and links to Hong Kong's strengths (finance, technology, links with mainland China). Keep current-event references factual and non-political.
- Scholarship applications are often separate forms with their own short-answer questions.

## United Kingdom (UCAS undergraduate)

- One statement goes to all (up to five) choices, so it must suit every course and should not name a single university.
- For entry from 2026, UCAS replaced the free-form statement with **three structured questions** within the same overall limit of **4,000 characters** (including spaces), with a minimum length per answer. Confirm the current questions and limits on the UCAS site.
- Strong emphasis on the subject itself (reading, projects, super-curricular activity) over personal life story.

## United States (undergraduate)

- **Common App** personal essay: up to 650 words, chosen from a set of prompts. Many colleges add supplemental essays with their own limits.
- Personal voice and story are expected; reflection matters more than listing achievements.
- "Why us" supplements need very specific details about that college.

## Canada, Australia, New Zealand (undergraduate)

- Many programs do not ask for a personal statement for direct-entry undergraduate admission; some competitive or professional programs do (supplemental applications, personal profiles). Check per program.

## Europe (motivation letters)

- Often a formal letter (date, address, "Dear Admissions Committee"), 1-2 pages.
- Direct and structured: background → motivation → fit → goals.
- See the formal letter template in `post-templates.md`.

## Mainland China, Japan, Korea, Taiwan (international programs)

- English-taught programs often ask for a study plan or statement of purpose, sometimes with set questions.
- A more modest, respectful register usually reads better than bold self-promotion.

## Graduate programs (all regions)

- Statement of purpose: research fit and preparation. Personal history only where it explains motivation.
- US graduate programs often separate "statement of purpose" and "personal history / diversity statement". Do not merge them.

---

## When the region is not covered

1. Ask the user for the official prompt and limits.
2. Search the university's admissions page.
3. Default to: clear structure, 500-650 words, specific reasons for the program, modest but confident tone.
