# What to Show, Field by Field

Each entry: what readers in the field look for, the evidence a school leaver and a graduate can offer, what to avoid, and one example sentence. Examples are illustrations only: never put them in a statement unless they are true for the applicant.

---

## Computer Science and Software

- **Look for**: building things, debugging, learning on one's own, understanding why code works.
- **School leaver**: a small program or website, what broke and how it was fixed, self-study.
- **Graduate**: a project with a measurable result, design choices and trade-offs, open-source or research work.
- **Avoid**: lists of languages with no use shown; "I have loved computers since I was young" with no moment.
- *Example*: "My quiz game crashed every time someone answered fast; finding the loop that caused it taught me more than the chapter on loops."

## Data Science and Statistics

- **Look for**: maths and statistics preparation, curiosity about questions, judgement about what data can and cannot show.
- **School leaver**: a spreadsheet or Python analysis of something real (a class survey, sports results), strong maths grades.
- **Graduate**: a dataset handled end to end, a model evaluated against a baseline, awareness of bias and leakage.
- **Avoid**: "data is the new oil"; claiming AI expertise after one tutorial.
- *Example*: "Our class survey said students who slept less scored higher, and it took me a week to work out what else was different about them."

## FinTech, Computational and Quantitative Finance

- **Look for**: quantitative ability plus real interest in how money systems work: payments, risk, fraud, markets, regulation.
- **School leaver**: a concrete trigger from daily life (QR payments, a family business, a scam message), maths and coding steps taken.
- **Graduate**: modelling or data projects in finance, understanding of regulation and risk.
- **Avoid**: get-rich motives, crypto hype, market-beating claims, unverified statistics.
- *Example*: "Every time we paid by QR code, something decided in a second that the payment was safe. I wanted to know what."

## Engineering

- **Look for**: applying theory under constraints, design trade-offs, teamwork, safety.
- **Evidence**: a build or design project, what failed, what was changed and why.
- **Avoid**: listing tools; claiming impact without numbers or tests.

## Business and Economics

- **Look for**: analytical thinking about real situations, initiative, leadership with results.
- **Evidence**: a small venture, a competition, a family business task, an economic question investigated.
- **Avoid**: "I want to be a CEO"; buzzwords.

## Medicine, Nursing and Health

- **Look for**: understanding of the reality of care, empathy shown through interaction, scientific interest, resilience.
- **Evidence**: shadowing or volunteering with reflection on a specific patient interaction (anonymised), science work.
- **Avoid**: "I want to help people" without a situation; heroic narratives.

## Law

- **Look for**: reasoning, reading closely, interest in how rules work in practice, fairness.
- **Evidence**: debate, mock trials, an issue followed closely, work or volunteering involving rules.
- **Avoid**: TV-drama motivation; grand justice claims.

## Humanities

- **Look for**: close reading, independent thinking, writing that shows the mind at work.
- **Evidence**: a text or question that changed how the applicant thinks, reading beyond the syllabus.
- **Avoid**: summaries of famous works without a personal argument.

## Social Sciences

- **Look for**: curiosity about people and institutions, method (how you would find out), data and evidence.
- **Evidence**: a small survey or observation, an issue in the applicant's community analysed.
- **Avoid**: opinion without evidence.

## Natural Sciences and Mathematics

- **Look for**: rigour, curiosity, persistence with hard problems, lab or proof experience.
- **Evidence**: an experiment, olympiad or problem-solving work, a concept that clicked and why.
- **Avoid**: "science is everything"; listing topics.

## Design, Architecture and Creative Fields

- **Look for**: a process (research, iteration, critique), a point of view, a portfolio that matches the statement.
- **Evidence**: one project from brief to result, what feedback changed.
- **Avoid**: describing taste without process.

## Education

- **Look for**: experience with learners, reflection on what worked, commitment to fairness.
- **Evidence**: tutoring, mentoring, making learning materials, with what changed for the learner.

---

## Changing or combining fields

Connect them through transferable skills and a real reason, not through dislike of the old field. Example structure: what the old field gave you → the question it could not answer → why the new field answers it → what you bring.
