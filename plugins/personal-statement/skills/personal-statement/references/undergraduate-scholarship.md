# School Leavers (Undergraduate) and Scholarship Statements

Most writing advice for personal statements is written for graduate and PhD applicants: labs, papers, supervisors. A student finishing secondary school has none of these, and that is expected. Readers judge them on curiosity, initiative, fit and potential.

---

## 1. What undergraduate readers look for

| They look for | Evidence a school leaver can give |
|---|---|
| Genuine interest in the subject | A concrete moment that started it; what the student did next because of it |
| Initiative beyond the classroom | Self-study (online courses, tutorials, books), small projects, competitions, clubs |
| Readiness for the course | Relevant school subjects and grades, especially maths for quantitative courses |
| Fit with this program and place | One or two real reasons tied to the course content or the city/industry |
| Character | Responsibility, persistence, working with others, shown through one small story |

Small things told specifically beat big things told vaguely. "I built a quiz game in Python that kept crashing until I learned what a loop index was" is better than "I am passionate about programming".

## 2. Structure that works (450-650 words)

1. **Opening moment** (60-90 words): the first spark, as a scene.
2. **Growth** (100-130 words): school experience → self-study → something made.
3. **Turning point / direction** (80-110 words): the conversation, event or realisation that pointed to this course.
4. **Why this course (and second choice) and this place** (100-140 words): shared core of the choices, one specific reason for the place, optionally one verified current fact.
5. **Plan and contribution** (60-90 words): foundations → projects or internships → direction. Scholarship line goes here if needed.
6. **Closing** (20-40 words): echo the opening, or one concrete next step.

Cut proportionally for shorter limits.

## 3. Common mistakes for school leavers

- The statement is mostly about the people who inspired them. Keep the inspiration, but make the applicant the main character.
- Listing everything ever done. Choose two or three experiences and go deeper.
- Writing like a graduate applicant ("my research interests lie in...").
- Over-formal vocabulary that does not match the rest of the writing.
- "Since I was a child I have always loved..." with no specific memory.
- Not mentioning the second-choice program at all.
- Technical mistakes about the subject (e.g. calling an editor or IDE a programming language). These hurt more in the field being applied for.

## 4. Scholarship statements

A scholarship panel asks: *will this money produce a good outcome, and is this person a good bet?*

### Do

- State need, if relevant, **once and plainly**: "The scholarship would let me study full-time without needing to work during term."
- Connect the money to a plan: what it frees the student to do.
- Show merit with evidence: grades, awards, projects, responsibilities.
- Show contribution: what the student will give back (mentoring, community, field), **only if true**.
- If the scholarship has stated criteria (leadership, community service, academic excellence), map one piece of evidence to each.

### Don't

- Beg ("I really hope", "please", "I promise").
- Over-dramatise hardship or use it as the only story.
- Promise contributions the applicant has no intention or history of making.
- Write a separate essay that contradicts the main personal statement.

### Combined admission + scholarship statement

When one statement serves both, keep scholarship content to one short paragraph or two sentences inside the "plan" paragraph. The rest stays about the subject and the fit.

## 5. Two programs in one statement (1st and 2nd choice)

1. Find what the two programs share (e.g. Data Science and FinTech share maths, statistics and programming).
2. Lead with the first choice.
3. Give the second choice one sentence that names its own appeal (e.g. "applied to the money systems people use every day").
4. Make the "why this place" reason work for both.
5. Never imply the second choice is only a fallback.
